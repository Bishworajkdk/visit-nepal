Stand with Nepal

What happened in the August 2026 flood, where you can still travel, and how to help.

On the morning of 26 August 2026, part of a glacier broke away near Langtang Lirung. The falling ice and rock became a fast, debris-laden flood that swept down the Bhote Koshi and into the Trishuli River, reaching settlements along about 72 kilometres of the valley within hours.

What happened

The flood destroyed the Rasuwagadhi border crossing with Tibet, the trading town of Timure and much of Syabrubesi, the gateway to the Langtang treks. Further downstream it struck Betrawati, Trishuli Bazar and Devighat. Rasuwa and Nuwakot were hit hardest, with damage also in Dhading, Gorkha and Chitwan.

By mid September, Nepali authorities had confirmed more than 1,300 deaths, and thousands of people were still missing. Around 8,300 homes were destroyed, along with schools, health posts, bridges, roads and 13 hydropower projects. The Nepali Army, Armed Police Force and Nepal Police led the rescue, joined by teams from many countries.

A warning from a warming mountain

Satellite images show the glacier had been speeding up for weeks before it failed. Scientists say rising temperatures are weakening the frozen ground that holds high Himalayan ice and rock together, though some stress that local geology also played a part. Nepal produces a tiny share of the world's emissions, yet it lives on the front line of a changing climate.

Travelling now

The flood struck one river corridor in the north. Kathmandu, Pokhara, the Everest region, the Annapurnas, Mustang and Lumbini lie outside the flood zone and are welcoming visitors.

Avoid the Langtang Valley, Gosaikunda and the Rasuwagadhi route until authorities say they are safe. Some highways along the Trishuli were closed or damaged, so check road conditions with your agency or the Nepal Tourism Board and allow extra time. Choosing to come still matters: many families who lost everything worked in tourism.

How to help

The Government of Nepal runs the Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund for rescue, relief and rebuilding. You can give from anywhere through its official site, run by Nepal Clearing House.

Donate to the relief fund

Visit Nepal does not collect or receive any donations. Your gift goes straight to the Government of Nepal. Donations cannot be refunded, so check the amount and currency before you confirm.
