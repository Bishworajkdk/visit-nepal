    When to go

    Pick a month to see the weather and festivals. Festival dates follow the lunar calendar and move a little each year.

    AutumnWinterSpringMonsoon

    Winter

    January

    Cold and clear. Snow on high passes, sunny days in Kathmandu, Chitwan and Lumbini.

    Festivals: Maghe Sankranti marks midwinter.

    Winter

    February

    Still cold in the mountains, pleasant in the lowlands. Quiet trails.

    Festivals: Losar, the Tibetan and Sherpa new year, often falls now.

    Spring

    March

    Warming up. Rhododendrons start to bloom in the hills.

    Festivals: Maha Shivaratri at Pashupatinath and Holi, the festival of colours, usually fall in late winter or March.

    Spring

    April

    One of the best trekking months. Forests in full flower.

    Festivals: Nepali New Year in mid April, with Bisket Jatra in Bhaktapur.

    Spring

    May

    Warm and hazier. Everest climbing season peaks.

    Festivals: Buddha Jayanti, the Buddha's birthday, is celebrated in Lumbini and at the great stupas.

    Monsoon

    June

    Monsoon arrives. Green and quiet, with rain most afternoons.

    Festivals: Rice planting season in the terraces.

    Monsoon

    July

    Heaviest rain. Landslides can close roads. Mustang and upper Manang stay drier.

    Festivals: A good month for the rain shadow treks.

    Monsoon

    August

    Rain continues. Leeches on forest trails.

    Festivals: Janai Purnima and Gai Jatra, a festival of remembrance in Kathmandu.

    Monsoon

    September

    Rain eases late in the month and the views return.

    Festivals: Indra Jatra fills Kathmandu Durbar Square, and the Kumari is carried through the streets.

    Autumn

    October

    The best month for most visitors. Clear skies and sharp mountain views.

    Festivals: Dashain, Nepal's biggest festival, usually falls in late September or October.

    Autumn

    November

    Dry, clear and cooler. Busy trails, so book lodges early.

    Festivals: Tihar, the festival of lights, and Mani Rimdu at Tengboche Monastery.

    Winter

    December

    Crisp and quiet. Cold nights in the mountains.

    Festivals: A calm month for the Kathmandu Valley and Chitwan.

    Travel essentials

    The practical things worth knowing before you land.

    Visas

    Most nationalities can get a tourist visa on arrival at Kathmandu airport and the main land borders, or fill in the form online before travelling. Indian citizens do not need a visa. Fees and rules change, so check the Department of Immigration before you go.

    Getting there and around

    Most visitors fly into Tribhuvan International Airport in Kathmandu. Gautam Buddha International Airport near Lumbini and Pokhara International Airport also take some international flights. Tourist buses link Kathmandu, Pokhara and Chitwan. Domestic flights save days but depend on the weather.

    Trekking permits and guides

    Most treks cross national parks or conservation areas and need an entry permit. Since April 2023, foreign trekkers in these areas must go with a licensed guide. A registered trekking agency can arrange both.

    Altitude

    Above about 3,000 metres, climb slowly, take rest days and drink plenty of water. If a headache, nausea or breathlessness gets worse, stop climbing and go down. Make sure your insurance covers helicopter rescue at the height you plan to reach.

    Money and phones

    The currency is the Nepali rupee. Cards work in cities, but carry cash for the mountains, where ATMs are rare. A local SIM from Nepal Telecom or Ncell is cheap and easy to buy with your passport.

    Health and safety

    Drink treated or filtered water, not tap water. Nepal is generally safe, but take care on mountain roads and check the weather before trekking.

    Respect and etiquette

    Dress modestly at temples and take off your shoes before entering a shrine or a home. Walk clockwise around stupas and prayer wheels. Ask before photographing people, and never photograph cremations up close. Give and receive with your right hand.

    Arts and crafts

    Much of Nepal's beauty is made by hand. Buying directly from the makers is one of the best ways to support local families.

    • Paubha and thangka painting पौभा

      Newar artists of the Kathmandu Valley have painted sacred scrolls for centuries, and their paubha style shaped the Tibetan thangka. Watch painters at work in the studios around Boudhanath and Bhaktapur, where a single scroll can take months.

    • Wood carving काष्ठकला

      Carved windows, struts and doorways are the signature of Newar architecture. The famous Peacock Window in Bhaktapur's Dattatreya Square shows what a master carver can do with a single piece of wood.

    • Pottery and metalwork माटो र धातु

      In Bhaktapur's Pottery Square, potters still throw clay on hand-turned wheels and dry it in the sun. In Patan, Newar craftsmen cast bronze statues using the lost wax method passed down through families.

    • Paper and cloth लोक्ता र ढाका

      Lokta paper is handmade from the bark of a Himalayan shrub and lasts for generations. Dhaka cloth, woven in bold patterns in the hills, is used for the traditional Nepali topi, or cap.

    Beliefs and culture

    Long before the great temples were built, the people of these hills looked to shamans, spirits and the sky. Much of that world is alive today.

    Ban Jhakri बनझाँक्री

    In Tamang tradition, the wild shaman of the forest carries off gifted children and teaches them to heal. Shamans across Nepal still honour him as their first teacher, and still play the dhyangro drum in healing rituals.

    Tantra तन्त्र

    A path shared by Hindus and Buddhists that seeks the divine within the body. In the Kathmandu Valley it shapes temple ritual, Newar Buddhism and the worship of the living goddess, the Kumari.

    Astrology ज्योतिष

    A family astrologer draws up a birth chart for most children. It guides their name, their marriage match and the lucky hour for a wedding, a new home or a long journey. Find your rashi
